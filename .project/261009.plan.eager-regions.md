# Plan: eager regions in the inline specification

## Goal

Stage 2 of [[261007.plan.inline-specification]]. Add to `Precedence.v`
the inline constructs that claim their bytes once they open: verbatim
(V1 to V4, P6), math (MA1), raw inline (R1), autolinks (A1 to A3) and
symbols (Y1). `para_inlines_valid` keeps its statement, and
`over_alphabet` admits the backtick, `$`, `<` and `:`.

Extensions stay off, as in stage 1: no dollar math, no holes, no tag
names. Attribute specs are stage 3, so `{` keeps its current condition
in `follow_ok`.

## Tokens now depend on the reading

`Precedence.v` lexes a paragraph once, from its bytes alone
(`para_tokens`), and a reading picks roles for those tokens. Stage 1
fits that shape. Stage 2 does not, for two reasons.

**A bracket region and an eager region can overlap.** In
`` [a](b`c)d` e `` the destination closes at the `)`, the backtick
inside it is source, and lexing resumes at `d`; the next backtick
opens a verbatim. In `` a](b `c) d` `` the same bytes after `](` are
read as text, the first backtick opens a verbatim, and the `)` is
inside it. Whether the `]` pairs is a fact about the reading (a `*`
pair around it can kill its `[`), so the token boundaries after a `](`
or `][` follow from the reading too. No single token list serves both.

**A lexed escape can straddle a region's end.** Inside a verbatim or
an autolink a backslash is a byte, so `lex`'s `TEsc`, `TEscWs` and
`THard` are wrong there: `` `a\`b` `` closes at the escaped backtick.
Lexing eager regions first fixes that case, since they are lexical
once their start is known. It does not fix the first problem.

Per-line lexing goes too: a verbatim crosses a line break, so the
paragraph is lexed as one string with the newline as a byte.

## Decision wanted

**D1. How `valid` indexes a reading.**

- **(a) Byte positions, recommended.** A reading's pairs and openers
  are byte offsets. `tok_at s p` lexes the one token at `p`; it is
  reading-independent, and an eager region is one token (a verbatim
  with its closing run and any raw spec, an autolink candidate, a
  symbol). The tokens a reading sees are a chain: from `p` to
  `p + length`, except after a `]` that pairs, where the chain jumps
  past the region (`region_end`, now on bytes: the destination machine
  stage 1 wrote, `dstep`, and the next unescaped `]` for a label). A
  position off the chain has no role, which is what `inert` says
  today. This matches the reference's own wording ("a region opens at
  a byte not inside another region") and fits stages 3 and 4: a spec
  that closes and a `[^` label are tokens of the same kind.
- **(b) A token list per reading.** A reading names the `]`s it takes
  as regions, and the tokens are `lex` given that set. The indices
  stay token indices but now depend on the set, so every lemma about
  positions carries the set. Same work as (a), harder to read.
- **(c) Restrict the alphabet.** Keep one token list, lex eager regions
  as tokens, and exclude from `over_alphabet` any backtick, `<` or `:`
  after a `](` or `][` up to where its region would end. Smallest
  change, but it leaves out the interaction the stage was meant to
  settle, and the theorem stays T~ on exactly that shape.

## Uniqueness on paper (under (a))

`valid_unique` is an induction on positions: two readings that agree
before `n` agree at `n`. It needs every condition at `n` to read only
the reading before `n`. With regions:

- Whether `n` is on the chain reads the pairs closed before `n`: a
  jump starts at a `]` before `n`, and its end is a function of the
  bytes.
- `tok_at s n` reads the bytes only, so an eager region's extent is
  fixed once its start is on the chain. No eager region opens inside
  another one, since its interior is off the chain.
- `inert` becomes "off the chain", and `behind` and `dead` are
  unchanged.

So `agree` gains one clause, the chain below `n`, and the step lemma
proves it at `n` from the pairs below `n`. The fixed point the stage
plan worried about does not arise, because the chain is built left to
right.

## Step 1 as built (2026-10-10)

The reading is on byte offsets of the paragraph, and the theorem keeps
its shape: `para_inlines_valid` now takes `valid (para_string ls)` and
gives `tree_of (para_string ls) m`. No token kind was added.

- **Tokens.** `para_string` joins the lines with a newline; `next_tok`
  is the one-token lexer and `tok_at s p` the token at `p`. `TBreak` is
  the newline. A line's token is the same read from the line alone
  (`next_tok_after`), which the simulation uses to stay per line.
- **The chain.** `chain_next s m p` is `p` plus the token's length, or,
  for a `]` that pairs, the byte after its region (`resume`); `chain` is
  its closure from 0. `valid` asks a closer and an opener to be on the
  chain, where stage 1 asked them not to be `inert`. Uniqueness needed
  one new lemma, `chain_agree`; `chain_between` gives that the chain is
  one increasing sequence.
- **M3 and its converse** are `tok_end s i < j` and `tok_end s p = j`,
  as step 0 found.
- **Regions on bytes.** `region_end` uses `dest_close` and
  `label_close`, which skip an escaped byte. `tree_of` takes a region
  in one step: a label as written, a destination through `dest_text`.
- **`ref_read` and `tree_of`** walk the chain with fuel fixed at the
  definition (`rgo`, `tgo`). `rinv` lost its mode fields; positions the
  chain skips are handled by `rinv_skip`, using `chain_gap`.
- **The simulation.** The view steps over the units the scanner sees: a
  chain token outside a region and a byte inside one (`punit`, the
  relation `pruns`). `tree_of_pruns` ties a whole walk to `tree_of`; the
  region's bytes are folded into one link by `region_walk`. In a region
  the scanner is followed byte by byte, so the old per-token region
  cases are gone. A destination reading that never closes is stated
  with its pending backslash (`never`), and one generic lemma
  (`dest_close_app`) replaces the token-level layer bookkeeping;
  `esc_off` is gone.
- **One change to the alphabet.** `over_alphabet` no longer lets a
  backslash escape a newline. A line holds none, and with the paragraph
  lexed whole an escaped newline inside a line would read differently
  from the scanner.
- Sizes: `Precedence.v` 1,570 to 1,720 lines, `InlinePrecedence.v`
  5,200 to 5,190. `make check-grammar` and the axiom check pass; the
  grammar enumerator reads `tok_at` and maps its indices to offsets.

## Step 2, verbatim, as built (2026-10-10)

Verbatim is in; math and raw inline are not.  `$` is outside the
alphabet, and so is `{` after a backtick, so neither can follow a
closing run.

- **Token.** `TVerb n body closed` is a run of `n` backticks and the
  bytes to the next run of exactly `n`, or to the paragraph's end
  (`verb_tok`, `verb_go`).  It is text to `valid` and `ref_read`, and
  `tree_of` makes it `Verbatim (trim_verb body)`.
- **Alphabet.** The backtick is in `in_alphabet`.  `follow_ok` keeps
  `{` out after a backtick, and `over_alphabet` keeps it out after an
  escaped backtick too: inside a verbatim the backslash is a byte and
  that backtick may close it.
- **The view.** A verbatim of more than one byte is walked byte by byte
  in a third mode, `PMAtom x e`, which adds the node at byte `e`.
- **The scanner inside a verbatim** is the scan of its bytes so far from
  the text state it began in (`linked`'s atom case).  `verb_state` says
  its closing run is complete only before a backtick, so a newline
  inside is a byte (`ibreak_verb`).  `verb_tok_scan` ties `verb_go` to
  the scanner's `IOpen` and `IVerb`, and `scan_atom` is the step.  After
  a closing run, the next byte (not a backtick, not `{`) settles the
  scanner into text.
- **Line ends.** A verbatim that closes at a line's end leaves the
  scanner holding its closing run; one the paragraph ends in leaves a
  state that finishes as the node.  Both are `vpending`, a third way a
  line may end beside `linked` and `pending`.  An unclosed verbatim can
  end on the newline before an empty last line, which `break_linked`
  allows for.
- **The grammar** walks byte offsets with `tok_at` instead of a token
  list lexed once: after a region the chain can resume inside what a
  straight lexing read as a verbatim.  `test/grammar.ml` has backtick
  pieces; 255,046 paragraphs, none differ.
- Sizes: `Precedence.v` 1,720 to 1,780 lines, `InlinePrecedence.v` 5,190
  to 6,040.

## Step 2, math, as built (2026-10-10)

- **Tokens.** While math is on, a run of dollars before a backtick run
  is part of the verbatim token: `TVerb pre n body closed` with `pre`
  the run's length.  One dollar is inline math, more are display math,
  and the dollars before the last two are text (`verb_node`, and the
  text `pstep` and `tstep` add first).  Any other run of dollars is one
  text token, `TDollars`.  A run is one token because the scanner holds
  it for two bytes (`IDollar`): `$$x` resolves only at the `x`, which
  the one-byte lookahead of a token (`settles`) cannot follow byte by
  byte.
- **Alphabet.** `$` is drawn on while dollar math is off.  Dollar math
  stays out, as an extension.
- **The simulation.** The verbatim lemmas take `pre`: `verb_tok_scan`
  starts from text through the dollars (`iscan_dollars`) into
  `IOpen m (vkind_of pre)`, and `verb_state` allows `IDollar` before a
  dollar or the backtick run.  A closing run settles into text on the
  next byte for math as for verbatim (`verb_close_settles`); a `$` after
  inline math is text because dollar math is off.  `scan_verb` is the
  step that opens a verbatim or math from text, for the backtick and the
  dollar alike.  `rres` resolves a pending `$` while dollar math is off,
  so a run that ends a line is text there.
- Sizes: `Precedence.v` 1,780 to 1,860 lines, `InlinePrecedence.v` 6,040
  to 6,410.  The grammar check has `$` pieces; 255,031 paragraphs, none
  differ.

## Step 2, raw inline, as built (2026-10-10)

- **Scope.** Only a raw spec that closes is in the alphabet, and only
  while raw inline is on: a `{` after a backtick, escaped or not, must
  begin `{=FORMAT}` with a nonempty format of bytes that are not
  whitespace, a backtick or a brace (`raw_spec`, `raw_ahead`).  A spec
  that fails is out, so the text it would leave never arises.  A `{=`
  after math is the highlight opener, as in the scanner.
- **Token.** `TVerb` carries the spec's format (`raw`), set when the
  verbatim has no dollars, closes, and a spec follows (`verb_tok`).  Its
  node is `RawInline` (`verb_node`).
- **The simulation.** After a verbatim's closing run the scanner reads
  the spec in `IRaw` (`iscan_raw_spec`), and the `}` leaves it in text
  with the node out, so the last byte needs no lookahead.  `verb_state`
  lets a closing run be followed by `{`, and an empty spec only by `=`;
  that keeps the attribute branch of the scanner out of reach.  A
  verbatim with no spec is not followed by `{`, which `scan_atom` gets
  from the alphabet and from the lexer having found no spec there.
- Sizes: `Precedence.v` 1,860 to 1,930 lines, `InlinePrecedence.v` 6,410
  to 6,750.  254,912 paragraphs with raw-spec pieces, none differ.

## Step 3, autolinks, as built (2026-10-10)

- **Token.** `TAuto src ok` is a `<` and the region after it, up to the
  byte that ends a candidate: `>`, whitespace, a second `<`, or the end
  of the line (`auto_stop`, `auto_go`).  It takes the `>` and is a link
  when the region is an address (`auto_body_ok`, `auto_kind_ok`);
  otherwise it is the text of the `<` and the region, and the byte that
  ended it is read on its own.  So a `*` inside a failed candidate
  delimits nothing, as the baseline table has it.
- **Alphabet.** `<` is drawn on, and a candidate's region holds no
  backslash (`auto_clean`): there a backslash is a byte, and the line's
  alphabet would read it as an escape.  `:` and `.` stay out, so the
  links in reach are emails (`x@y`); a URL needs the `:` of step 4.
- **The simulation.** A candidate never crosses a line, so it is one
  token in text mode, read by `scan_tok` like any other (`scan_auto`):
  the scanner is in `IAuto` over the region (`iscan_auto`), a link's
  `>` leaves it in text, and a failed candidate settles into text on the
  byte that ended it.  `rres` resolves a candidate the line ends inside
  into its text.
- Sizes: `Precedence.v` 1,930 to 2,010 lines, `InlinePrecedence.v` 6,750
  to 6,950.  254,988 paragraphs with `<` pieces, none differ.

## Costs and risks

- Step 1 touches most of `Precedence.v` (1,570 lines) and the index
  arithmetic of `InlinePrecedence.v` (5,200 lines). The scanner is
  already simulated byte by byte, so `n` counting tokens becoming `n`
  counting bytes may simplify `scan_sim`; I have not checked how far
  that holds.
- The verbatim token spans lines, and the simulation steps line by
  line through `ibreak`. A verbatim open at a break is an `IVerb` state
  carried across, which the per-line induction has to allow.
- Region tokens are single tokens to `valid`, but the scanner builds
  them byte by byte with its own states (`IOpen`, `IVerb`, `IRaw`,
  `IAuto`, `ISymbol` with its shadow). Each needs a small simulation of
  its own, entered from `ilead`.
