# Plan: the inline specification

## Goal

Step 1 of the move from coverage to specification
(`syntax-reference-coverage.md`): state inline syntax as a reading of a
paragraph that does not mention the scanner, and prove the scanner
builds its tree.  Today 35 inline rules are at E ("An example is
enough"); each stage below turns a group of them into instances of a
stated rule.

## Shape: widen `Precedence.v`, one stage at a time

`Precedence.v` already has the three layers a specification needs, and
`InlinePrecedence.v` the agreement:

1. **Tokens** (`next_tok`, `tok_at`): the token at a byte of the
   paragraph, with the flanking rules (M2) read off neighbouring bytes.
2. **Readings** (`valid`): which tokens pair, as the reference words it
   (first closed wins, closest opener, marked with marked), at byte
   offsets along the reading's chain (`chain`): a destination or label
   after a `]` that pairs is source, and the chain jumps past it.
   `valid_unique`: at most one reading.
3. **The tree** (`tree_of`).

The agreement is `para_inlines_valid`, for paragraphs `over_alphabet`.
Each stage adds token kinds to the alphabet, cases to `valid` and
`tree_of`, and cases to the simulation in `InlinePrecedence.v`; the
theorem keeps its statement and loses restrictions.  At the end
`over_alphabet` admits every byte of djot with the extensions off, and
P1 to P5 are T.

The alternative, a new module designed for the whole language at once,
would not reuse the 3,900 lines of simulation and would land nothing
until it is finished.

## The grammar alongside (2026-10-08)

`inline-grammar.md` states the same rules as a grammar over the tokens,
run by `dev/InlineGrammar.v` and checked against `ref_read` by `make
check-grammar`.  It is not proved.  Each stage extends it with the
stage's rules and keeps the check at zero differences; writing the rules
there first is a cheap sketch of their shape.  Its equivalence with
`valid` is proved once, after the last stage.

## What the probes say about regions (2026-10-07)

Both engines agree that verbatim, math, autolinks, symbols and attribute
specs, once opened at a byte that is not already source, claim the bytes
up to their end, delimiter closers included:

| Input | Both engines |
| --- | --- |
| ``*a `b* c` `` | `*a <code>b* c</code>` |
| `*a <http://x*> b*` | strong around the autolink |
| `_a :sym_bol: b_` | emphasis around the symbol |
| `_a {.c_} b_` | emphasis; the spec is claimed, `_}` with it |
| ``[a `b](x)` c`` | the verbatim claims the `](` |

So these are regions in the sense of `inert`, but opened by their own
opener rather than by a `]` that pairs.  Two regions are ours by
adjudication and differ from djot.js: a destination that closes is
source even if a backtick inside it would open verbatim (2026-08-13), and
a `[^` label that closes is source even if a closer inside it would pair
(2026-08-15).  The specification states ours.

## Stages

Each is its own plan when started.

1. **Escapes and breaks** (O2 to O5).  A backslash and the byte it
   escapes are one text token; before a newline, a hard break; before a
   space, a non-breaking space.  An escaped delimiter character is no
   longer a delimiter.  Smallest stage: no regions, and it takes the
   backslash out of the excluded bytes.
2. **Eager regions** (V1 to V4, P6, MA1, R1, A1 to A3, Y1).  A region
   opens at a byte not inside another region and ends where its own
   grammar says: the next backtick run of the same width or the end of
   the paragraph, a `>` on the same line with a URL or email between, the
   next `:` after a symbol word.  `inert` gains these regions; they
   interleave with bracket regions, which is the case to get right.
3. **Attributes and spans** (AT1, AT6, AT7, C2, N1).  A spec's extent
   and attributes are `AttrSyntax.body` and `attrs_of`
   (`AttrAgree.v`), so this stage depends on step 2 being done.  What a
   spec attaches to, stacking, `]{` as a bracket closer, and a spec
   cannot begin with a delimiter character (`a{-k=v}`).
4. **The other brackets** (I1, F1, L4, L6).  `![`, `[^` as a region,
   `][` with an empty label.
5. **Smart quotes** (Q1 to Q3).  The reference does not state the
   direction heuristic (Q1: "Djot is pretty good about figuring out from
   context"); stating it is a clause beyond the reference, written from
   the code and checked against djot.js.
6. **Text** (Q4, Q5, B1, H1, S1, D1, M1, M4, M5, L1 to L3): mostly
   instances once the alphabet admits their bytes, plus dashes and
   ellipses as text rewriting.

## Risks

- The simulation in `InlinePrecedence.v` is the expensive part, and
  each stage reopens it.  Probe each new token kind against the scanner
  on generated paragraphs (`make probe-lemmas`) before proving.
- `valid` may stop being unique once regions open themselves; a region
  opener inside another region must be inert, which is a fixed point
  over positions.  Check uniqueness on paper for stage 2 before
  building on it.
- Smart quotes may resist a declarative statement short of transcribing
  the heuristic.
