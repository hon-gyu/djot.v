# Plan: lazy lines meet container uniformity

## 0. Goal

Prove what a document with lazy lines means, for each container the
djot syntax reference lets a paragraph line skip the prefix of: block
quotes, list items and footnotes.  The target shape, one theorem per
container:

```
parse ["> a"; "b"]      = [BlockQuote (parse ["a"; "b"])]
parse ["- a"; "b"]      = [bullet list, one tight item holding parse ["a"; "b"]]
parse ["[^n]: a"; "b"]  = [footnote n holding parse ["a"; "b"]]
```

stated over any text lines `a` and `b`, not these two strings.

## 1. Where things stand (branch `hy/lazy-line-in-container-uniformity`)

Two families of theorem exist and do not yet meet for every container.

- Uniformity (`Uniformity.v`, `ListUniformity.v`): a container's contents
  parse as they would at top level, *when every line carries the
  container's prefix*.  `quote_uniformity` builds its input with
  `map (fun x => "> " ++ x)`; `list_uniformity` with `list_lines`, which
  indents continuation lines by the marker's width; the footnote theorems
  require every line blank or indented past the opener.
- Lazy lines (`Step.v`, `Uniformity.v`, commits `13ef1d2`, `b49fe28`):
  `step_lazy_restore` says a text line continuing an open paragraph gives
  the same step as the line with `spine_prefix` in front;
  `lazy_line_restore` lifts that to documents; `lazy_stack_line` states it
  over `lazy_stack`, the reference's list of containers.

Chaining them: restore every lazy line's prefix (lazy theorem), then read
the fully prefixed document (uniformity).  That works only if the prefix
`spine_prefix` writes is one the uniformity theorem accepts, and if the
contents come out in the shape the right-hand side of the goal names.

| Container | `spine_prefix` writes | Uniformity accepts | Contents come out as | Joins? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| block quote | `> ` | `> ` on every line | `parse ["a"; "b"]` | yes |
| list item (`- `) | 1 space | the marker's width, 2 spaces | `parse ["a"; "b"]` | no: ` b` is not `  b` |
| footnote | 1 space | any indent past the opener | `parse ["a"; " b"]` | no: the indent stays in the contents |

The parser treats ` b`, `  b` and `b` the same as a paragraph
continuation line, since they all land in the item and `feed_lazy` and
the paragraph branch both drop leading whitespace.  No theorem says so
yet.  That is the whole gap.

## 2. Steps

### Step 1. Any valid indentation, not only the minimum

Generalize the spine lemma from one spelling to every spelling.  Replace
the single string `spine_prefix col st` with a relation
`spine_spelling col st p`: `p` is a prefix the open containers accept in
front of a continuation line.

- quote: `> ` followed by a spelling for the inner state at `col + 2`;
- list item or footnote at column `ind`: `blanks k` for any `k` with
  `col + k > ind`, followed by a spelling for the inner state at
  `col + k`;
- div, pending attributes, key: a spelling for the inner state;
- paragraph: `""`.

Prove `step (p ++ l) st = step l st` for every `p` with
`spine_spelling 0 st p`, under the premises `step_lazy_restore` already
takes, and `spine_spelling 0 st (spine_prefix 0 st)` so the existing
theorem is one instance.  `step_fuel_spine` already pushes a run of
spaces through a list or footnote with `step_fuel_pad`; the change is
that `k` is quantified rather than fixed at `S ind - col`.

This is the statement the parser satisfies, not a choice of spelling,
so it is preferred over the alternative of making `spine_prefix` write
the formatter's indentation (which would also need the marker's width in
`list_state`, which records only its column).

### Step 2. A paragraph ignores its continuation lines' indentation

For the footnote row: `parse (x :: p ++ y :: rest)` equals
`parse (x :: y :: rest)` when `x` opens a paragraph, `y` is text that
does not underline, and `p` is blank.  This is `step_lazy` at
`st = PPara _`, with `classify_ws_prefix`, `feed_lazy_ws_prefix` and a
`bunderline_of` whitespace lemma (the pad proofs in `Step.v` already
use one).  Small.

### Step 3. One worked theorem per container

In `Uniformity.v` next to `lazy_line_restore`, over lines `a` and `b`
with `classify a = KText`, `keyless a = true`, `classify b = KText`,
`bunderline_of b = None`:

- `quote_lazy_line`: `parse ["> " ++ a; b] = [BlockQuote (parse [a; b])]`,
  from `lazy_line_restore` and `quote_uniformity`.
- `list_lazy_line`: `parse ["- " ++ a; b]` is the one-item tight bullet
  list of `parse [a; b]`, from step 1 at `k = 2` and `list_uniformity`.
- `footnote_lazy_line`: `parse ["[^n]: " ++ a; b]` is the footnote
  holding `parse [a; b]`, from step 1, `footnote_text_uniformity` and
  step 2.

These are the checked form of the chaining argument in §1; without them
it is only a comment.  Keep them to one lazy line under one container.
Nested containers and several lazy lines follow by the same chaining and
do not each need a theorem.

### Step 4. README

The container-uniformity table's lazy-line row cites the three
corollaries, and says in one sentence that together with the uniformity
rows they give the meaning of a document with lazy lines.
