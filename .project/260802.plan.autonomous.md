# Plan: a verified djot in Rocq

The body below is kept **evergreen**: it says what the plan is now, and it
is edited in place when reality moves. Every edit is recorded in the
[Changelog](#changelog) at the end, so no delta lives in the body itself.

`ai-disclosure` stays `autonomous`, which describes the bulk of the plan
as originally written. Later edits are prompted and reviewed; the
changelog says which are which.

Execution plan for the research note in
`reference/Formalizing djot in Rocq.v2.md`. The note says *what* and *why*;
this says *in what order*, with exit criteria and decision points. Effort
labels assume part-time work; treat them as relative sizes, not dates.

## Goal

A djot parser written in Gallina, with machine-checked proofs of djot's
design commitments (prefix determinism → uniformity, locality of inline
classification, no-backtracking [^1], termination/totality, roundtrip), extracted
to OCaml and differential-tested against both `djot.js/` and `djoths/`
(submodules in this repo). Stretch goal: parameterize the parser by an
extension-knob record and prove a composition (frame) lemma — the research
contribution.

## Non-goals

- Verifying djot.js or djoths themselves; they are oracles.
- HTML rendering fidelity, filters, CLI.
- A linear-time complexity proof. The djot README makes this a design
  goal, but monotone source consumption alone does not establish it; an
  explicit cost model and any needed amortization remain deferred.

## Phase 0 — Infrastructure and de-risking spikes

Everything later depends on this loop existing: edit Gallina → extract →
run corpus → diff against oracles.

Deliverables:

1. Repo skeleton: `theories/` (Rocq, dune-built), `extraction/` (OCaml
   driver around the extracted parser), `harness/` (differential test
   runner).
2. Corpus adapter: parse djot.js's `.test` file format
   (`djot.js/test/*.test`) and drive all three parsers over it. Output: a
   three-way diff report per case.
3. Oracle baseline: run djot.js's corpus through djoths and record every
   existing disagreement in `.project/oracle-disagreements.md` *before* any
   Gallina exists. These are spec data, not our bugs, and knowing them early
   prevents chasing ghosts later.
4. Spike A: transcribe `djoths/src/Djot/Parse.hs` combinators to Gallina and
   prove termination of `many`/`some` under a consumes-or-fails discipline.
   This is the known Rocq pain point; if it fights back, switch to fuel
   (spec-sanctioned: nesting limits are explicitly allowed) and record the
   decision.
5. Spike B: extraction round-trip of a toy parser through the full harness.

Exit criteria: `make test` runs a trivial Gallina parser against one `.test`
file and prints a three-way diff. Spike A resolved with a written decision
(measure vs fuel).

Decisions to make here:

- **Rocq version and stdlib** (Rocq 9.x + Stdlib vs MathComp — default:
  plain Stdlib; nothing here needs MathComp).
- **String model**: byte-level (`list byte` / ByteString-style), matching
  djoths. UTF-8 validity handled as a predicate, not baked into the type.
- **AST source of truth**: transcribe `djoths/src/Djot/AST.hs`, diffing
  against `djot.js/src/ast.ts` for field-level disagreements; record any in
  the disagreement log.

## Phase 1 — Well-formed AST and roundtrip

1. `wf_block` / `wf_inline`: well-formedness as indices on an inductive
   family (no empty paragraphs, no empty block lists, canonical terminal
   blank-line ownership, …). Well-formed-by-construction replaces
   generator-filtering predicates.
2. Djot renderer in Gallina, modeled on `djoths/src/Djot/Djot.hs`.
3. Roundtrip statement: `parse (render d) = d` for `d : wf_block`. Where
   several renderings are valid, prefer canonicalizing the AST type; fall
   back to a Narcissus-style encoding relation only if canonicalization
   distorts the AST.
4. Test the *renderer* differentially too: render random `wf_block`
   inhabitants, feed to both oracles, check they parse back to the same
   tree.

## Phase 2 — Block parser and prefix determinism

1. Block parser in Gallina using djoths's `BlockSpec` shape: a record of
   `start` / `continue` / `close` / `finalize` per container, dispatched
   over a container stack. Keep the record shape from day one — Phase 4
   parameterization depends on it.
2. Termination: lexicographic measure over (input position, stack depth),
   or fuel per the Phase 0 decision.
3. **Prefix-determinism lemma**: block *tree shape* is a function of line
   prefixes, computable line by line with no future-line dependence. Stated
   over tree shape only — the pipe-table retroactive-header rule falsifies
   any stronger statement over full parse results.
4. Uniformity as a corollary, quantified over all `BlockSpec` entries:
   container contents parse as they would at top level. One proof, every
   container — including future ones.
5. Hook the extracted block parser into the harness; drive the block-level
   subset of the corpus.

Exit criteria: prefix determinism and uniformity proven over the full
`BlockSpec` table; extracted parser agrees with oracles on block structure
for the corpus (inline content compared as raw text at this stage).

## Phase 3 — Inline parser, locality, no-backtracking

1. Inline parser formalizing **djot.js's algorithm**, not djoths's: one
   left-to-right pass, an opener stack, resolution by annotating an earlier
   match (`djot.js/src/inline.ts`). djoths's `<|>`/lookahead style does
   not expose the structural no-replay argument directly; use djoths only
   as a cross-check here.
2. **No-backtracking theorem**, in the sense fixed in
   [[no-backtracking]]: no consumed region is later resubmitted to
   tokenization under a different interpretation.
   Not "scanner position strictly increases", which is a stronger claim
   that also condemns the opener stack's retroactive annotation, and that
   is not backtracking. State it over *what the scan re-reads*, not over
   the shape of the state updates. `reparseAttributes` is a known
   violation in djot.js's implementation, but its observable recovery
   can be represented by a product state without replay.
3. **Locality theorem**: inline classification is a function of the inline
   text alone — parsing the same inline sequence under different
   reference/note maps yields ASTs differing only in resolved targets,
   never structure. Scoped to classification; auto-identifiers, implicit
   heading references, and attribute transfer stay in a separate
   whole-document resolution pass, kept out of the theorem statement.
4. Attribute parser: transcribe `djoths/src/Djot/Attributes.hs` (already an
   explicit state machine in both implementations; cheap).
5. Small combinatorial specs, as standalone decidable functions with
   uniqueness proofs: hyphen division (em/en/hyphen preference), list-type
   ambiguity resolution (`i.` vs alpha, resolved to continue the list),
   smart-punctuation quote direction (formalize the implemented rule; the
   prose spec has none).
6. Full corpus through the harness; adjudicate every three-way disagreement
   into the log: our bug / oracle bug (file upstream) / spec gap (record).

Exit criteria: no-backtracking and locality proven; corpus disagreements
all adjudicated (zero *unexplained* diffs — explained, logged divergences
are acceptable).

## Phase 4 — Knob-indexed parser

Precondition (audit before committing, ~1 wk): each candidate knob must be
expressible as a localized change — one `BlockSpec` field, one delimiter-set
entry, one decision point — not a thread through control flow. Any knob that
fails the audit gets redesigned or dropped from the verified set. If most
fail, stop here; Phases 1–3 stand alone.

1. `Config` record threaded through the parser (family-based style: one
   parser, proofs quantified over the record). Djot's shipped behavior is
   one distinguished inhabitant; Phase 3's theorems become corollaries at
   that point.
2. Name the structural invariants `I` explicitly: prefix determinism,
   single-pass inline scanning, delimiter-run disjointness, blank-line-only
   paragraph termination, locality. Re-prove `I ⟹ P` once, config-generic.
3. Per-knob obligations `preserves k I`, starting with provably-disjoint
   trigger sets (cheap wins), then the two known overlap clusters: emphasis
   delimiters and line-prefix rules.  Here `preserves` is the ordinary
   invariant-preserving endomorphism statement: `forall c, I c -> I (k c)`.
   `disable_row_preserves_admissible` is the unconditional first instance.
   The general `update_drow_preserves_admissible` uses `preserves_when`: its
   local compatibility check asks that the replacement row is valid and its
   trigger differs from every unchanged enabled row.

Exit criteria: djot-default settings provably satisfy each applicable `I`;
every exported field-local update primitive has a preservation theorem for
each applicable invariant; and both known overlap clusters have executable
positive and negative compatibility witnesses.

This replaces the provisional “≥ a dozen knobs” count. The implementation
has four genuine update primitives: delimiter-row replacement, delimiter-row
disabling, marker interruption, and underlining. Counting the nine delimiter
targets as nine different knobs would reward aliases rather than coverage;
the generic row theorem is strictly stronger than nine copied corollaries.
The revised criterion is met: delimiter and lone-dash compatibility cover the
two overlap clusters, while inline single-pass/locality and block
incrementality are parametric in every admitted setting.

## Phase 5 — Frame lemma and proof-guided refinement — complete

Invariant-preserving knobs compose automatically: `preserves k₁ I ∧
preserves k₂ I ⟹ preserves (k₁ ∘ k₂) I`; this is
`Config.preserves_compose`.  The earlier plan put `disjoint k₁ k₂` in that
theorem, but the hypothesis is redundant once each knob preserves `I` for
every admitted input configuration.  Trigger disjointness belongs one level
earlier, as a decidable sufficient condition for proving a local edit
`preserves`. `drow_update_compatible` and
`update_drow_preserves_admissible` now realize that step for the delimiter
table. `bmarker_update_compatible` and `bunderline_update_compatible` do the
same for the one block-prefix overlap: a lone dash cannot be both a bullet
marker and an underline. Then the
payoff for the extension question: for a restrictive
djot choice, extract the weakest precondition from the `I ⟹ P` proof and
exhibit a strictly more permissive knob setting with the same theorem
(worked target: paragraph interruption by unambiguous-marker constructs
only — more permissive than djot, provably immune to the accidental-list
problem that motivated the ban).  The worked target is now
`Invariants.accidental_list_immune`: its exact local precondition is
`marker_interrupt_precondition`, and
`with_marker_interrupts_accidental_list_immune_iff` proves necessity as well
as sufficiency.  `prose_safe_markers` is the maximal policy satisfying it;
`sublist_bconfig` is strictly more permissive than djot's never-interrupt
policy, while the unrestricted policy is rejected by the `1865.` witness.

**Read Schwerdfeger & Van Wyk (PLDI 2009) before designing Phase 4's
`Config`** — their isolation condition should shape what a "trigger set" is,
even though their LALR setting doesn't transfer directly.

The phase is complete by both checkpoints originally proposed:
`Config.preserves_compose` is the frame lemma, and
`Invariants.accidental_list_immune` with `prose_safe_markers` is the
proof-guided knob refinement beyond djot. A stronger theorem connecting a
general class of local trigger checks to all exported parser properties would
be a new research direction; it is not unfinished work under this milestone.

[^1]: What "no backtracking" means here

The current source-based interpretation is developed in
[[no-backtracking]]. What is forbidden is committing to an
interpretation, then rewinding and **re-reading** that input under a
different one. Lookahead, a collect-then-resolve pass split, revising
annotations on structure already built, and product states whose
alternatives advance together are all allowed.

So the test is re-reading, not rewinding in the loose sense. The opener
stack splicing `+emph` over a
placeholder and dropping spanned openers (`inline.ts:698-737`) re-reads
nothing, so it is not backtracking, and a theorem phrased as "scanner
position strictly increases" would wrongly condemn it.
`reparseAttributes` re-feeds consumed slices (`inline.ts:637`), so it is,
but that diagnoses djot.js's algorithm rather than proving its output
requires backtracking.
