# Plan: open candidates as a stack, not a chain

## Goal

Linear time for an unclosed link destination and an unclosed hole, on
any input, in the extracted parser, and proved to agree with the
specification scanner. This fixes item 2 under "Open" in [[holes]]
and the `[a](b` chain recorded there, together.

## As built (2026-10-07)

Option B below, smaller than proposed in two ways. The sections after
this one are the proposal as reviewed.

- **No shared raw buffer and no offsets.** A frame keeps `cf_under`, the
  source from the opener of the frame below it to its own, and the
  stack keeps `s_seg`, the source since the top frame's opener. A byte
  is pushed once; a close joins the segments above the closing frame
  (`spop`). Everything is a `TextOps` buffer, so no new string type and
  no slicing.
- **No lifting.** A candidate becomes a frame only when the reading
  has just opened it (empty payload, depth 0, no pending escape) and
  its counter is above the frame below (`speel`). Anything else stays
  nested in `s_cur` exactly as the specification has it. That covers
  the chains in a bounded candidate's shadow, so the payload prefix and
  the chain-to-frames conversion of option B were never needed. Such a
  chain still costs what it costs on main.
- One frame list, top first, with the two kinds mixed. `s_dtop` and
  `s_htop` give the topmost level of each kind, and each frame saves
  the pair from before its push, so a pop restores them.

## Why it is quadratic

`IDest` and `IHole` each carry `sh`, the ordinary reading of their
region, and that reading opens the next candidate inside itself. k
open candidates are a chain k deep, and `istep_at` walks the whole
chain on every byte. Each link also copies the byte into its own
payload buffer (`dst`, `src`).

The other states that carry a shadow are bounded. `IAttr` steps its
shadow with attributes off, so no attribute candidate opens inside
another. `ISymbol` lives only while the alias is alphanumeric. Dollar
math closes at the next unescaped `$`. None of them is changed here.

## The two facts that collapse the chain

Taken from [[261007.plan.holes]] ("Linear time") and extended to
destinations:

1. Two candidates of the same kind count the same byte (`(`/`)` for a
   destination, `{`/`}` for a hole) under the same escape rule. If C2
   opens inside C1's ordinary reading, C1's depth stays at least one
   above C2's from C2's opener on. So C2 closes first, and if C2 never
   closes neither does C1.
2. When any candidate closes, its ordinary reading is discarded, and
   with it every candidate opened after it, of either kind.

The escape state is the same for every open candidate: it resets after
every byte that is not a backslash, and every opener byte (`(` after
`]`, `{` after `%`) is such a byte. So one paren counter, one brace
counter and one pending-backslash flag serve every frame. Payloads
come from one shared raw buffer: a hole keeps it raw and `hole_text`
decodes it; a destination's `dst` is a fixed decoding of the raw bytes
(`\c` to `c` for punctuation, otherwise kept; a break as `\n`).

## Design: a refinement, not a new specification

The specification scanner `iscan_g` and its five proof files stay as
they are. A new file, `theories/InlineStack.v`, defines a second
machine and proves that it computes the same thing. This is the
pattern `InlineBuffer.v` already uses for `chunks`.

```
sscan := {| s_dests : list dframe      (* top first *)
          ; s_holes : list hframe      (* top first *)
          ; s_parens s_braces : nat     (* shared counters *)
          ; s_esc : bool
          ; s_raw : Buf                 (* bytes since the oldest frame *)
          ; s_seq : nat                 (* next frame's opening order *)
          ; s_cur : iscan_g' |}         (* the one ordinary reading *)
```

- `dframe` holds what the `IDest` arm needs at its close (`kids`,
  `image`, `open`, `o`), the paren level and raw offset at its opener,
  and its opening order. `hframe` holds `txt`, `o`, the brace level, the
  offset and the order.
- `iscan_g'` is the reading. See "Open question" for what it is
  inside the shadows of the bounded candidates.
- On a byte: step `s_cur` once, then update the counters and `s_esc`.
  An unescaped `)` at the top destination's level closes it: pop it,
  pop every hole frame opened after it (two stacks plus opening order
  make that amortized O(1)), and set `s_cur` to the `IDest` arm's
  result on the frame's snapshot. The same holds for `}` and the top
  hole. A `](` or `%{` reached by `s_cur` pushes a frame instead of
  nesting a state.
- At a line break and at the end of the paragraph, frames turn into
  what the specification does with them: a break appends to `s_raw`;
  the end selects `s_cur`.

### The theorem

An abstraction `abs : sscan -> iscan_g` rebuilds the chain: the oldest
frame outermost, each frame's `depth` read off the shared counter and
its payload decoded from `s_raw`. Under an invariant `swf` (levels
strictly increasing within each stack, the counters at or above every
level, offsets in range, `s_cur` free of `IDest`/`IHole`):

```
swf s -> abs (sstep_at a c s) = istep_at a c (abs s)
swf s -> abs (sbreak_at a s)  = ibreak_at a (abs s)
swf s -> sfinish s            = ifinish (abs s)
swf s -> swf (sstep_at a c s), and the same for break
```

with the located cursor threaded through unchanged. From these follow
`para_inlines_at` and `parse_inline_line(_located)` equal to their
stacked versions, which is the substitution extraction makes.

### Where extraction changes

Today the per-line drivers are native (`Extract.v`, the `chunks`
loops), and the state crosses each line as a specification `iscan`.
That crossing would rebuild the chain on every line (`abs` costs the
chain), so the boundary moves up: `para_inlines_at`,
`parse_inline_line` and `parse_inline_line_located` are extracted as
the stacked machine, and the native line loop is rewritten against
`sscan` (its `IText false` fast path appends a run to `s_cur` and to
`s_raw`). The block parser only calls those three, so nothing above
the inline layer moves.

## What it costs and what it does not fix

- The five proof files and `dev/check/Hole.v` are untouched. The new
  proof is the simulation: the cases of `istep_at` that reach a frame,
  and a frame lemma (`istep_at` on the rebuilt chain is the chain of the
  stepped parts) used for all the others.
- **Closed nesting stays quadratic in what it closes.** Each close
  copies its payload out of `s_raw`, and in `[a](`ⁿ `)`ⁿ every inner
  link is built and later discarded by the one around it, so the work
  is the sum of the payloads. That is an O(n²) memcpy rather than
  today's O(n²) chain walk with copying, so it is much faster, but it is
  not linear. Making it linear needs payloads that stay slices until
  the paragraph ends, which changes the node type. Proposed as out of
  scope.
- The wasm limit of about 2000 inline nodes per paragraph ([[holes]],
  "Found on the way") is a stack depth issue and is separate.

## Open question: chains inside a bounded candidate's shadow

`IAttr`, `ISymbol` and dollar math are bounded in how many are live at
once, but their shadow can still grow a chain. `x{k="` followed by
`[a](b` repeated keeps the attribute candidate open (a quoted value can
be long) while destinations nest in its shadow. Two ways to handle it:

- **A. Stacks all the way down.** `iscan_g'` is a copy of `iscan_g`
  without `IDest` and `IHole`, whose shadows hold an `sscan`. A shadow
  that is selected merges its frames into the enclosing stack, rebasing
  levels and offsets once. Linear everywhere, but it duplicates the
  scanner's step function (about 400 lines of `InlineScan.v`) for the
  new type, and `abs` and the proofs follow it.
- **B. Stack at the top only.** `s_cur` is a specification `iscan_g`
  with no `IDest` or `IHole` at its head. Inside a bounded candidate's
  shadow the chain stays as today. When that shadow is selected, its
  head chain is lifted into frames, once per frame. A frame then keeps
  its payload so far as a prefix, since a destination's decoded `dst`
  cannot give back the raw bytes. Much less code; the quadratic case
  remains only while an attribute, symbol or math candidate stays open
  over many destinations or holes.

Recommendation: B first. It covers every shape in the table, and A can
replace it later without changing the theorem's statement.
