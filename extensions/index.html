Extensions

Syntax the parser reads in addition to djot’s. Every extension is off in the djot profile and is switched on by itself. For djot’s own syntax, see the djot syntax reference.

Each section ends with the properties that the extension changes.

Callouts

Setting: ext_callouts.

A block quote whose first line is a header [!kind] is a callout, as in Obsidian.

> [!note] Backlinks
> A backlink is a link seen from its target.

<div class="callout" data-callout="note">
<div class="callout-title">Backlinks</div>
<div class="callout-content">
<p>A backlink is a link seen from its target.</p>
</div>
</div>
Open in the playground

The header

The header is tested on the line that opens a quote, and only there. After the quote prefix it is:

  1. [!;
  2. a kind: one or more ASCII letters, digits, - or _;
  3. ];
  4. optionally a fold marker, + or -;
  5. then the end of the line, or a space or tab and the title.

The kind is kept as written. The title is the rest of the line, parsed as inline content.

> [!warning]- Do *not* rename
> The fold marker makes it a `details` element.

<details class="callout" data-callout="warning">
<summary class="callout-title">Do <strong>not</strong> rename</summary>
<div class="callout-content">
<p>The fold marker makes it a <code>details</code> element.</p>
</div>
</details>
Open in the playground

A later line of an open quote is not tested.

> a
> [!note] T

<blockquote>
<p>a
[!note] T</p>
</blockquote>
Open in the playground

What is not a header

Anything else after the ] leaves the line as djot reads it. This keeps links, spans and reference definitions that begin with [!.

> [!note](x)

<blockquote>
<p><a href="x">!note</a></p>
</blockquote>
Open in the playground 
> [!no te] T

<blockquote>
<p>[!no te] T</p>
</blockquote>
Open in the playground

The body

The following lines of the quote are the body, parsed as blocks. No blank line is needed after the header. A line without > directly after the header closes the callout, because the header opens no paragraph for it to continue.

> [!note] T
after

<div class="callout" data-callout="note">
<div class="callout-title">T</div>
<div class="callout-content">
</div>
</div>
<p>after</p>
Open in the playground

Inside the body, a paragraph continues onto a line without > as in any quote.

> [!note] T
> para
lazy

<div class="callout" data-callout="note">
<div class="callout-title">T</div>
<div class="callout-content">
<p>para
lazy</p>
</div>
</div>
Open in the playground

Output

Obsidian’s classes. data-callout holds the kind. With a fold marker the outer element is details, open for + and closed for -. The tree keeps a node of its own, with the kind, the fold marker, the title and the body.

Custom tag names

Setting: ext_tags.

A div and a span can have a name, which becomes the element.

Press :kbd[Ctrl+C] to copy.

<p>Press <kbd>Ctrl+C</kbd> to copy.</p>
Open in the playground 
::: details
Hidden until opened.
:::

<details>
<p>Hidden until opened.</p>
</details>
Open in the playground

This extension changes how existing djot documents parse: in djot the word after a div fence is a class, and :name[ is text before a bracket.

A div’s name

The word after a div’s opening fence is its name. Which lines open a div does not change. A fence with no word opens an unnamed div.

{.note}
::: aside
A named div with a class.
:::

<aside class="note">
<p>A named div with a class.</p>
</aside>
Open in the playground

A span’s name

:name[ opens a named span. The name is made of the characters a symbol :name: may contain. The span closes at the first ] that arrives while it is the innermost open bracket, and nothing after the ] is waited for.

:kbd[x](u)

<p><kbd>x</kbd>(u)</p>
Open in the playground

Attributes attach as they do to any inline.

:kbd[Esc]{.key}

<p><kbd class="key">Esc</kbd></p>
Open in the playground

Where :name[ is not a named span

After an escaped colon, or when the colon directly follows a letter, a digit or a colon.

\:kbd[a] and a:kbd[b]

<p>:kbd[a] and a:kbd[b]</p>
Open in the playground

When it never closes.

:kbd[a

<p>:kbd[a</p>
Open in the playground

Output

The name is the element name when it is made of ASCII letters, digits and hyphens, starts with a letter, and is not an element whose content a browser reads differently (a void element such as br, or script, style, textarea, title, iframe and similar). Otherwise the element is div or span and the name is in a data-tag attribute.

:script[x] and :a_b[y]

<p><span data-tag="script">x</span> and <span data-tag="a_b">y</span></p>
Open in the playground

Dollar math

Setting: ext_dollar_math.

Math between dollar signs, as in pandoc’s Markdown and on GitHub. It is independent of djot’s own math syntax, $`x`, which stays available.

The area is $\pi r^2$.

<p>The area is <span class="math inline">\(\pi r^2\)</span>.</p>
Open in the playground

Two dollars give display math.

$$\int_0^1 x\,dx$$

<p><span class="math display">\[\int_0^1 x\,dx\]</span></p>
Open in the playground

Inline math

One $ opens inline math when the character after it is not whitespace and not a backtick. The next $ closes it when the character before it is not whitespace and the character after it is not a digit. Otherwise the opener is text.

It costs $5 and $10.

<p>It costs $5 and $10.</p>
Open in the playground 
To split $100 in half, we calculate $100/2$.

<p>To split $100 in half, we calculate <span class="math inline">\(100/2\)</span>.</p>
Open in the playground

Inside the math, a backslash and the character after it are kept as written, and that character cannot close.

$a\$b$

<p><span class="math inline">\(a\$b\)</span></p>
Open in the playground

The content is not parsed as inline syntax.

$a*b*c$

<p><span class="math inline">\(a*b*c\)</span></p>
Open in the playground

Display math

Two $ open display math, closed by the next $$. The whitespace and digit conditions do not apply. Three or more $ in a row are text.

$$x$ and $$$y$$$

<p>$$x$ and $$$y$$$</p>
Open in the playground

The GitHub form

A $ directly before a code span and another directly after it make inline math whose content is the code span’s.

$`x_1`$

<p><span class="math inline">\(x_1\)</span></p>
Open in the playground

Priority

A closer has to arrive before the end of the paragraph, heading or table cell. Once math closes, it wins over emphasis, links and spans that overlap it.

*a $b* c$

<p>*a <span class="math inline">\(b* c\)</span></p>
Open in the playground

A $ inside a code span is not an opener, and \$ is a literal dollar.

`a $b` c$ and \$x$

<p><code>a $b</code> c$ and $x$</p>
Open in the playground

Holes

Setting: ext_holes.

A hole is an expression in a document, for a consumer to evaluate: the document is a template and the hole is where a value goes, as in an article template literal (Crichton and Krishnamurthi, “A Core Calculus for Documents”, POPL 2024). The parser records the expression as written and never reads it. What language it is in, how it is evaluated, and whether its value is a node, a list or an option are the consumer’s.

Today I am going shopping for %{List.length items} things.

<p>Today I am going shopping for <code data-hole="">List.length items</code> things.</p>
Open in the playground

The HTML renderer shows a hole nobody evaluated as its expression, so it is not lost silently.

Where a hole ends

%{ opens a hole. Inside, every { must be matched by a }, and the } that matches the opening one closes the hole.

%{ {r with x = 1} }

<p><code data-hole=""> {r with x = 1} </code></p>
Open in the playground

A brace that should not count is escaped. \{, \} and \\ are read as {, } and \; a backslash before any other character is kept with it, so the expression’s own escapes reach the consumer as written.

%{ print_string "\}\n" }

<p><code data-hole=""> print_string "}\n" </code></p>
Open in the playground

A hole may cross a line break, which is part of the expression. It ends with its paragraph, heading or table cell like any inline.

a %{ f
x } b

<p>a <code data-hole=""> f
x </code> b</p>
Open in the playground

What is not a hole

A hole is always closed. When no closing } arrives before the end of the block, there is no hole and the source reads as if the % had been escaped. Here the first %{ never closes, so the second one opens a hole.

a %{x and %{y} b

<p>a %{x and <code data-hole="">y</code> b</p>
Open in the playground

An empty expression is still a hole, for the consumer to reject.

%{}

<p><code data-hole=""></code></p>
Open in the playground

Without the setting, %{.c} is a % with attributes. With it, %{ is always a hole’s opener, and \% writes a % before braces.

a %{.c} b and \%{.c} c

<p>a <code data-hole="">.c</code> b and <span class="c">%</span> c</p>
Open in the playground

A % inside a code span, an autolink or a link destination is not an opener.

`%{x}` and [a](%{u})

<p><code>%{x}</code> and <a href="%{u}">a</a></p>
Open in the playground

Priority

Once a hole closes, it wins over emphasis, links and spans that overlap it: its content is not markup.

*a %{b* c}

<p>*a <code data-hole="">b* c</code></p>
Open in the playground

Between a hole and a code span, whichever opens first wins: a code span reads its content as text, and a hole that closes reads its own.

`a %{b` c} and %{ f "`" }

<p><code>a %{b</code> c} and <code data-hole=""> f "`" </code></p>
Open in the playground

Blocks

There is no block syntax. A code block whose language is % is a code block to the parser; a consumer that evaluates holes may read it as one, with its content kept exactly, blank lines and indentation included.

``` %
ul (List.map item items)
```

<pre><code class="language-%">ul (List.map item items)
</code></pre>
Open in the playground

Keyed blocks

Setting: ext_keyed.

A colon pairs a label with one block.

status: in progress

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>status</dt>
<dd>
<p>in progress</p>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground

When nothing follows the colon, the block is the one on the following lines.

steps:
- one
- two

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>steps</dt>
<dd>
<ul>
<li>
one
</li>
<li>
two
</li>
</ul>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground

This extension changes how existing djot documents parse: every line it reads as a key is a paragraph line in djot.

Where a colon makes a key

A text line is split at its first colon that is

  • not escaped;
  • not preceded by whitespace;
  • followed by a space or by the end of the line (a tab does not count);
  • outside every inline construct: not in a code span, a bracket, an attribute or an open delimiter. 
see http://example.com: not this colon, but the next

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>see http://example.com</dt>
<dd>
<p>not this colon, but the next</p>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground 
`a: b` is code

<p><code>a: b</code> is code</p>
Open in the playground 
foo : bar

<p>foo : bar</p>
Open in the playground

The label is one inline element: a run of text, or an emphasis, a link, a code span.

The value

What follows the colon on the key line is inline content, never block syntax.

foo: - bar

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>foo</dt>
<dd>
<p>- bar</p>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground

A line is split once.

foo: bar: baz

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>foo</dt>
<dd>
<p>bar: baz</p>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground

One block

A key takes one block, and its scope ends where that block ends. A paragraph ends at a blank line, a list at a line that is not part of it, a fenced block at its closing fence.

foo:
bar

baz

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>foo</dt>
<dd>
<p>bar</p>
</dd>
</dl>
<p>baz</p>
Open in the playground

Keys nest by line.

foo:
bar: baz

<dl class="keyed">
<dt>foo</dt>
<dd>
<dl class="keyed">
<dt>bar</dt>
<dd>
<p>baz</p>
</dd>
</dl>
</dd>
</dl>
Open in the playground

Where a colon is not a key

Lines that djot reads as something other than text are read that way first: a div fence :::, a definition list marker, a reference definition [x]: u, a footnote definition, a heading.

# foo: bar

<section id="foo:-bar">
<h1>foo: bar</h1>
</section>
Open in the playground

Output

A description list with one term and the class keyed. The tree keeps a node of its own, with the label and the block.

Setext headings

Setting: ext_setext_headings. On in the Markdown-like profile.

A paragraph followed by a line of = is a level 1 heading.

A heading
=========

<section id="A-heading">
<h1>A heading</h1>
</section>
Open in the playground

A line of two or more - makes a level 2 heading.

A heading
--

<section id="A-heading">
<h2>A heading</h2>
</section>
Open in the playground

A single - is a list marker and does not underline.

A paragraph
-

<p>A paragraph
-</p>
Open in the playground

The underline applies to the paragraph above it, so the heading can span several lines.

A heading
on two lines
===

<section id="A-heading-on-two-lines">
<h1>A heading
on two lines</h1>
</section>
Open in the playground

Without a paragraph above, the same line is what djot reads.

===

<p>===</p>
Open in the playground

Wikilinks

Setting: ext_wikilinks.

A wikilink is a target between double brackets, as in Obsidian.

See [[Backlinks]].

<p>See <a href="Backlinks">Backlinks</a>.</p>
Open in the playground

A | separates the target from an alias, which is the text shown.

See [[Backlinks|the other direction]].

<p>See <a href="Backlinks">the other direction</a>.</p>
Open in the playground

A ! in front makes it an embed, as it makes a link an image.

![[diagram.png]]

<p><img alt="diagram.png" src="diagram.png"></p>
Open in the playground

Where it opens and closes

A wikilink opens at a [ that comes directly after another [, and closes at the first ]] on the same line. A single ] is part of the target.

[[a]b]]

<p><a href="a]b">a]b</a></p>
Open in the playground

It does not cross a line break. One that is not closed by the end of its line is text.

[[a
b]]

<p>[[a
b]]</p>
Open in the playground

Target and alias

The region is split at its first |. Both parts are kept as written: nothing is trimmed, a backslash is not decoded, and the alias is not parsed as inline content.

[[a|*b*]]

<p><a href="a">*b*</a></p>
Open in the playground

An empty target is not a wikilink.

[[]] and [[|b]]

<p>[[]] and [[|b]]</p>
Open in the playground

What it changes in djot

A [ at the start of a link’s text is read as a wikilink opener. So a link whose text begins with a bracket reads differently.

[[1]](u)

<p><a href="1">1</a>(u)</p>
Open in the playground

In a table, the row is split into cells first, so a | inside a wikilink ends the cell.

Output

A wikilink renders as the link it stands for: the target is the href, and the alias, or the target when there is none, is the text. An embed renders as an image. The tree keeps a node of its own, with the target, the alias and whether it is an embed.
